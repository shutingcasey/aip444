# Source Credibility Report: Health Canada Indoor Air Quality Infographic

## Executive Summary
The source "Infographic: Protecting your indoor air" published by **Health Canada** is a **highly credible** resource. It provides evidence-based public health advice on mitigating the impact of outdoor air pollutants (such as wildfire smoke) on indoor environments. As an official government publication, it is subject to rigorous internal review and reflects current scientific consensus.

## 1. Source Overview
*   **URL:** [https://www.canada.ca/en/health-canada/services/publications/healthy-living/infographic-protecting-indoor-air-outdoor-pollutants.html](https://www.canada.ca/en/health-canada/services/publications/healthy-living/infographic-protecting-indoor-air-outdoor-pollutants.html)
*   **Main Claims:**
    *   Indoor air quality is heavily impacted by outdoor pollutants and indoor combustion sources.
    *   Effective protection strategies include using HEPA-filtered air cleaners, maintaining CO alarms, and sealing windows during poor outdoor air events.
    *   Avoiding ozone-generating devices is critical for respiratory health.
*   **Author:** Health Canada (Government of Canada).
*   **Publication:** Official website of the Canadian Federal Government (Canada.ca).

## 2. Author and Publication Investigation
*   **Author Credentials:** While no individual byline is provided, the content is produced by the **Water and Air Quality Bureau** within the Safe Environments Directorate of Health Canada. This bureau is tasked with researching and communicating the health effects of indoor air contaminants.
*   **Publication Reputation:** Canada.ca is the official domain of the Canadian government. It is a primary source for health regulations, scientific guidelines, and public safety alerts. Health Canada is globally recognized for its conservative, evidence-based approach to health risk assessment.
*   **Editorial Process:** Government publications undergo a multi-stage review process involving subject matter experts (toxicologists, epidemiologists), legal review, and communications editing to ensure accuracy and accessibility.

## 3. Evidence and Corroboration
The claims made in the infographic are standard public health recommendations supported by multiple independent organizations:
*   **Corroboration:** The advice to use HEPA filtration and close windows during smoke events is echoed by the **National Collaborating Centre for Environmental Health (NCCEH)** and the **U.S. Environmental Protection Agency (EPA)**.
*   **Transparency:** The page links directly to technical guidance for professionals, Residential Indoor Air Quality Guidelines (RIAQGs), and ozone-safe device lists (CARB), providing a clear trail to the underlying data.
*   **Contradiction:** No credible sources were found that dispute the effectiveness of HEPA filters or the danger of carbon monoxide and ozone in indoor settings as described in the source.

## 4. Bias and Transparency
*   **Bias:** The tone is neutral, instructional, and focused on public safety. There are no commercial affiliations or advertisements.
*   **Transparency:** The source is highly transparent about its purpose (public health education) and its funding (publicly funded government agency). Contact information for the specific department (air@hc-sc.gc.ca) is provided for further inquiry.
